### 6. Definition of Ready (a story can enter a sprint when...)

- It is written as a user story with a clear benefit.
- It has acceptance criteria.
- It is small enough to finish within one sprint.
- Any dependency (data, access, approval) is known.

### 7. Definition of Done (a story is done when...)

- Code works as described in the acceptance criteria.
- It has been tested manually on the happy path and the main error cases.
- It works on the browsers used at the company.
- Database changes are applied and checked in SQL Server.
- It has been demonstrated to my mentor and feedback recorded.
- The board and logbook are updated.

## Part B: Reflection

**What I did.** At the start of the placement I met with my mentor to understand what the portal needed to do. I turned that conversation into this charter so that we shared the same understanding of the purpose, scope and how we would work together, including weekly demos.

**What I learned.** Before this placement, I treated a project as "just start coding". Writing the charter taught me that the biggest risks are usually about people and expectations, not code. Agreeing the Definition of Done early stopped us from arguing later about whether something was finished. Weekly demos also kept the project honest, because my mentor could see real progress and correct me quickly. As the only developer, I also learned that I need working agreements with *myself* (a self-review checklist and daily written stand-up).

**What was difficult.** Working agreements are easy to write and harder to follow. When I was busy building, I sometimes skipped the daily note. I noticed that this made the demos harder to prepare for.

**What I would do differently.** I would revisit the charter at the end of every sprint instead of treating it as a one-off document, and I would capture the "out of scope" list with my mentor more explicitly in week one.

**How this links to my studies.** This connects to what I learned in Project Management and Systems Analysis about stakeholders, scope and requirements, and it showed me why those theories matter in a real workplace.
