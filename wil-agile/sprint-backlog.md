## Part B: Reflection

**What I did.** Each week I selected a small group of items from the product backlog, broke them into tasks with hour estimates, set a sprint goal, and finished the week with a demo and a short retrospective.

**What I learned.** Planning around my real capacity (about 34 hours a week, and not all of it for building) made my commitments realistic. A sprint goal helped me decide what to drop when something took longer than expected. The retrospective was small but valuable. Writing one action per sprint was more effective than a long list of ideas that I would not follow up.

**What was difficult.** Estimating testing and bug-fixing was the hardest part. These tasks can grow, and there is no team to share the load, so a surprise bug affects the whole sprint.

**What I would do differently.** I would record actual hours for every task from the first sprint so I could compare them against my estimates, and I would plan a small buffer in every sprint for unplanned work.

**Link to my studies.** This applies Scrum concepts such as the sprint goal, sprint review and retrospective in a real workplace with a real Product Owner.
